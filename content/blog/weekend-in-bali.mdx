I booked the flight six days before I went. I did not book accommodation until I was already on the plane, using the inflight wifi to find a place with good reviews and a rice paddy view for under Rp 500,000 a night.

This is not my usual approach. Usually I have spreadsheets.

## Arriving Without a Plan

The guesthouse was run by a woman named Ibu Kadek who had five cats, strong opinions about coffee, and a ritual for every hour of the morning. She was up at 4:30 to make offerings. By the time I woke up at 6:30 the courtyard already smelled like incense and frangipani.

Breakfast was served on a wooden table overlooking a narrow terrace that dropped down through banana trees into a small valley. I sat there for two hours. There was no wifi.

## The Wrong Turn

On the second day I rented a bicycle and set off to find a waterfall I'd seen mentioned on someone's blog. I had a screenshot of a map but the roads don't always match the maps here. I turned wrong twice and ended up on a dirt track through a village where children ran alongside the bike for about a hundred meters.

I never found the waterfall.

I found instead: a family compound making traditional offerings for a temple ceremony, a man who insisted I stop and try jackfruit he was pulling off a tree, and a stretch of road through old-growth banyan trees where the light came through in panels.

> The wrong turn is often the thing worth writing home about.

## On Slow Travel

I used to plan trips like projects — deliverables, milestones, optimal routes between attractions. I've been unlearning this for a while now. The Ubud trip was a test of the unlearning.

What I found is that days without an agenda tend to expand. Not in a mystical sense — practically. You notice things when you're not moving toward a specific thing. The quality of shadow in the afternoon. The sound of a gamelan practice drifting over a wall. The particular orange of turmeric in a market stall.

None of this is original insight. But it doesn't stop being true just because other people have said it.

## What I'll Remember

Not Tegalalang (though yes, I went, the rice terraces are genuinely beautiful). Not the yoga class I tried once. Not the restaurant where every tourist goes.

I'll remember Ibu Kadek waking me up at 5 AM on the last morning because there was something I "must see." She walked me out to the courtyard where a procession was passing on the road — white and gold, moving slowly, drums, about forty people in a line.

"This is not for tourists," she said, not unkindly. "But you may watch."

I watched.
